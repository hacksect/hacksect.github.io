Background

My Cybersecurity Journey

My interest in cybersecurity began in 2020, just after secondary school, when I fell victim to a phishing attack. My bank card details were compromised and unauthorized transactions were made on my account. It was a painful experience, and it became the starting point of my journey into cybersecurity.

I began learning on my own through free online courses, including Introduction to Cybersecurity by Simplilearn, and joined online communities such as HackerAce, where I started as a writer, producing articles and online safety guides. Through the community I discovered hands-on platforms like TryHackMe and Hack The Box. That convinced me to build a career in the field, and I sat the UTME to study Cybersecurity at the Federal University of Technology, Akure.

Along the way I was fortunate to be mentored by Dr. Adebunmi A. Akinbo, who guided and supported me even though cybersecurity is not his specialty. A member of the Internet Society (ISOC), he introduced me to the ISOC Nigeria Chapter, which I joined in 2022. Together we ran cybersecurity awareness campaigns for secondary schools and universities, which built my confidence and strengthened my passion for community outreach.

He also registered me for the (ISC)² Certified in Cybersecurity exam, which I passed on my first attempt in January 2023. Financial constraints have so far kept me from obtaining the certificate. The journey has had its challenges, but it has taught me persistence.

With the support of the ISOC Nigeria Chapter, I have since led a team in Akure delivering awareness sessions in secondary schools on safe internet practices and responsible digital citizenship. Today I work as an Ethical Hacking Tutor, mentoring aspiring cybersecurity professionals through hands-on labs, and I founded ELIO (Education, Learning, Information & Online Security), an initiative offering security awareness training, ICT risk advisory, and security policy development.

Beyond technical security, my interests include Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC), cybersecurity policy, digital governance, and cyber resilience. I want to use cybersecurity to strengthen organizations, support secure digital transformation, and contribute to policies that promote trust, privacy, and responsible technology adoption.

I believe cybersecurity is as much about people as it is about technology. Through community engagement and awareness work, I am committed to making cybersecurity knowledge accessible, so that individuals and organizations can navigate the digital world safely.

01 Lab Simulation

Ethical Hacking Lab — Full Penetration Test Simulation

2025 – 2026

Designed and operated an isolated virtual lab simulating a full-cycle penetration test against a deliberately vulnerable environment. Built from scratch using VirtualBox with Kali Linux as the attacker and Metasploitable 2 as the target, on a host-only network to ensure safe, contained testing.

What was done

Host discovery and full port scan across all 65,535 ports
OS fingerprinting, banner grabbing, and service version detection
Enumerated SNMP, NetBIOS, NFS, SMTP, and DNS services
Identified vulnerabilities using Nmap scripts, Nikto, ZAP, and Metasploit
Produced professional VA report with CVSS v3.1 scored findings

02 Web App Security

Web Application Security Assessment — DVWA

2025 – 2026

Performed a structured web application penetration test against DVWA (Damn Vulnerable Web Application) using OWASP ZAP configured as a proxy. Mapped all findings against the OWASP Top 10 (2021) and produced a full report including an executive summary for non-technical stakeholders.

Key findings

SQL injection identified and validated
Cross-site scripting (XSS) — reflected and stored
Broken authentication and session management weaknesses
Cross-referenced ZAP and Nikto output to identify false positives
CVSS severity ratings assigned to all findings

03 Infrastructure Security

Rate-Limiting with a Traefik Reverse Proxy

2024

Group school project (with course mates) demonstrating how rate limiting protects services from abuse and traffic flooding. Built a Docker-based setup with an Nginx container serving a static website behind Traefik acting as a reverse proxy, then observed and documented traffic behaviour under load to show the practical difference between protected and unprotected endpoints.

What was demonstrated

Deployed an Nginx container serving a static site, using Docker
Configured Traefik as a reverse proxy in front of it
Implemented and tested rate limiting controls under load
Documented traffic behaviour comparison: protected vs unprotected
